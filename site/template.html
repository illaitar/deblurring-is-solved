Data: the test sets in one format

The frames every method is scored on. GoPro, REDS and RealBlur, released under CC BY 4.0, are on Hugging Face as lossless FFV1 with a CRC on every frame: 6.9 GB instead of 28 GiB of the authors' archives (python tools/testsets.py download). The other sets state no license for copies; their cards link to the authors' release.

  • Every released checkpoint of a method runs on every test set: video methods on the four video sets, image methods also on RealBlur-J, RealBlur-R, GS-Blur and MIORe, which hold single images. Trained on picks the checkpoints shown: GoPro (the default), the training split of the current test set, any set, or one training set.
  • GS-Blur: the 1,000 pairs of the authors' mini release, with the noisy blurred input (input_noise), each image at its own size. MIORe: only its training part has public sharp frames, so the test set is 300 of the 7,860 public training pairs, evenly spaced over the sorted file list. Both are scored like the other sets, without alignment. The GoPro checkpoints of NAFNet diverge on part of these images (outputs far outside the valid range, a PSNR of 3 to 6 dB, the same in fp32 on the CPU). Those images stay in the scores.
  • On RealBlur, PSNR and SSIM follow the dataset's official evaluation: each output is aligned to the sharp image by an ECC homography and scored on the valid region (scikit-image 0.17.2, OpenCV 4.2, as the script requires). LPIPS and the other metrics use the unaligned output.
  • PSNR and SSIM are computed per frame on 8-bit RGB outputs against the sharp frame and averaged over all frames of the set. LPIPS uses the AlexNet backbone.
  • A score is final when at least 95 % of the frames of the set are scored. Runs below that are shown under unfinished runs.
  • Each row is one variant of a method: the whole frame at once, tiles, test-time local converter (TLC), or the temporal scheme of a video method.
  • Compute (GMAC) and latency are measured per output frame at 1280×720, batch 1, on one A100. A method that needs a window of frames to emit one frame is charged for the whole window.
  • Frame characteristics come from the sharp frame and its blurry input: blur as the PSNR of the input and the share of gradient left after blur, texture, brightness, contrast, clipped highlights, input noise (Immerkaer) and colorfulness. Motion on the four video sets is the optical flow between consecutive sharp frames (MEMFOF). Every frame with its characteristics and every method's score on it is in results/frames/bench.sqlite.

05{{tag_dbis_big}}{{tag_dbisf_big}}

our versions of the methods

dbis gives the authors' outputs from plain PyTorch and a safetensors file, every method in the same layout with an encoder that loads on its own. dbisf is faster and lighter, and its mean PSNR is never more than 0.05 dB below the authors' code on any test set. What changed in each method: DBIS.md.

{{dbis_table}} 
import dbis
net = dbis.load("dbisf-BSSTNet")       # every model the same way; the weight file comes from Hugging Face on first use
out = net.restore(frames)              # options: tile, tile_overlap, window, batch, precision, compile, out

# the files: https://huggingface.co/Illaitar/dbis, or made from the authors' checkpoints
python -m dbis.convert NAFNet-64 NAFNet-GoPro-width64.pth          # -> weights/dbis/
python -m dbis.convert BSSTNet BSST_gopro.pth raft-things.pth      # BSST and its RAFT
python -m dbis.convert DSTNetPlus-L DSTNetPlus_L_gopro.pth
